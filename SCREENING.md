# Screening: Office Joke Open

Brief criteria, in order: (1) office life, works as a joke not a complaint;
(2) laugh earned, turn pays off in the last few words; (3) specific office
detail; (4) fresh, familiar setup taken somewhere unexpected; (5) reads cleanly
in one pass, no cuttable word.

Method: find the seam first (two frames colliding), punch word last, specific
nouns, cut everything after the trigger. First drafts discarded on sight.

## Candidates

### 1. "HR asked for my emergency contact, so I put down the printer repair hotline."
- (1) Office: HR form, yes. Joke, not grumble.
- (2) Turn: "emergency contact" sets up a person; "printer repair hotline" snaps to a machine. Payoff lands on the last two words.
- (3) Specific: HR onboarding form + the printer repair hotline, a number every office worker knows by heart.
- (4) Fresh: emergency contact and printer repair have not been paired before.
- (5) 76 chars, every word earns its place.
- Score: 5/5.

### 2. "The reply-all chain has 47 people in it, and 46 of them are asking to be removed from the reply-all chain."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: the removal requests ARE the chain, payoff on the repeated phrase. (3) Specific: reply-all. (4) Reply-all jokes exist, but the 46-of-47 count is a fresh turn. (5) 121 chars, "in it" is cuttable, the repeated tail is the joke but slightly baggy.
- Score: 4/5. Loses to #1 on tightness and punch-word placement.

### 3. "The office printer only jams on documents marked urgent, so now I label everything 'not urgent'."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: the workaround is the punch, lands late. (3) Specific: printer jams + "urgent" label. (4) Printer-jam jokes are well worn; the relabelling twist is only mildly fresh. (5) 99 chars, "now" is cuttable.
- Score: 3.5/5.

### 4. "The office fridge holds a sandwich from 2019, and the note on it says 'DO NOT TOUCH' in my handwriting."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: "in my handwriting" reframes the whole scene, last words. (3) Specific: office fridge archaeology. (4) Fresh angle, fridge jokes usually blame a coworker, not the speaker. (5) 109 chars, tight.
- Score: 4/5. Strong, but the laugh is recognition more than a snap; #1 snaps harder.

### 5. "The fire drill is the only meeting where everyone arrives on time and nobody takes notes."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: "nobody takes notes" reframes a drill as a meeting, late payoff. (3) Specific: fire drill + meeting notes. (4) Fresh pairing. (5) 92 chars, no waste.
- Score: 4/5. Loses to #1: the turn is gentler, more observation than punch.

### 6. "I asked IT for a second monitor and got a second meeting about monitors instead."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: "about monitors instead" lands last, misdirect from hardware to meetings. (3) Specific: IT ticket + monitor request. (4) Fresh-ish. (5) 88 chars, tight.
- Score: 4/5. Good, but the payoff word is "instead", a weak final beat next to "hotline".

### 7. "The meeting room is named 'Focus' and nobody in there has focused since 2021."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: "since 2021" dates the failure, late payoff. (3) Specific: named meeting rooms. (4) Mildly fresh. (5) 84 chars.
- Score: 3.5/5. Reads closer to a remark than a joke; the turn is a date, not a snap.

### 8. "My calendar invited me to a meeting called 'quick sync' and the agenda is a 47-slide deck."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: "47-slide deck" contradicts "quick", late. (3) Specific: calendar invite + slide deck. (4) "Quick sync" jokes are common; the deck count helps but not enough. (5) 95 chars.
- Score: 3/5.

### 9. "I set my status to 'in a meeting' during a meeting about updating our meeting statuses."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: recursion, payoff at the end. (3) Specific: status field + status meeting. (4) Fresh shape, but meta-recursion reads clever rather than funny. (5) 89 chars.
- Score: 3/5. Cleverness without a laugh.

### 10. "The printer printed my resignation letter before I finished typing it, and honestly, it had a point."
- (1) Office: yes, but edges toward complaint territory. (2) Turn: "it had a point" lands last. (3) Specific: printer + resignation letter. (4) Fresh. (5) 110 chars.
- Score: 3/5. The resignation frame makes it a grumble with a punchline, and criterion 1 penalises that.

### 11. "Our team standup lasts longer than the work we stand up to discuss."
- (1) Office: yes, but this is a complaint wearing a joke's clothes. (2) Turn: weak, the second half restates the first. (3) Specific: standup. (4) Stale shape. (5) 73 chars.
- Score: 2/5.

### 12. "I replied-all to say 'please remove me from this thread' and got 46 replies saying the same thing."
- (1) Office: yes. (2) Turn: the replies ARE the problem, late payoff. (3) Specific: reply-all. (4) Same family as #2, weaker count. (5) 103 chars.
- Score: 3.5/5. #2 does this shape better.

## Winner

**#1: "HR asked for my emergency contact, so I put down the printer repair hotline."**

Why it wins on criteria order: it is unambiguously an office joke, not a
complaint; the laugh is earned by a clean misdirection that pays off in the
final two words; the detail is concrete (HR form, printer repair hotline);
the pairing is fresh; and at 76 characters no word can be cut without loss.
